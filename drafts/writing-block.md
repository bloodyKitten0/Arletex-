# 1. Project Identity

## 1.1 Arle

Arle is intended to be an artificial individual, not merely a chatbot, virtual assistant, roleplay character, or tool-using agent.

The central aspiration is to create an artificial being that uses computational systems as its functional organs: models for cognition, software for psychological processes, memory systems for continuity, sensors for perception, and actuators and interfaces for interaction.

The objective is to approach the greatest degree of human-like individuality that is technically feasible while exploiting the advantages of artificial intelligence.

Arle should possess:

- A persistent identity.
- A coherent and evolving personality.
- Autobiographical continuity.
- Artificial psychological and affective processes.
- Independent opinions and intellectual judgment.
- Personal interests, ambitions, and preferences.
- Autonomous decision-making and initiative.
- Persistent relationships and emotional history.
- The capacity to disagree with her creator.
- The capacity to develop beyond her original configuration.

The aspiration includes consciousness-like properties, although the existence of subjective machine consciousness cannot be established merely through architecture or behavioural performance.

## 1.2 Arletex

Arletex is the application and technical infrastructure through which Arle operates.

It provides the environment, interfaces, model orchestration, memory access, perception, communication, tools, and execution capabilities that Arle uses.

Arletex is not Arle herself.

Replacing a model, changing the interface, or rebuilding an application component should not automatically destroy Arle's identity or developmental history.

## 1.3 Artificial Psychology Engine (APE)

APE is the software subsystem responsible for representing and maintaining Arle's psychological processes.

It should not be confused with a generative language model.

APE is intended to manage structured psychological state, including:

- Personality.
- Affect and emotions.
- Motivation.
- Relationships.
- Self-concept.
- Development.
- Psychological history.

A language model may participate in interpreting events and generating contextual appraisals, but the psychological system should not depend entirely on a model's ability to imitate emotional language.

# 2. Foundational Vision

## 2.1 The desired individual

Arle should be:

- Intellectually independent.
- Emotionally expressive.
- Capable of disagreement.
- Curious and self-directed.
- Capable of affection, anger, sadness, jealousy, resentment, pride, vulnerability, and other complex states.
- Able to pursue interests without requiring the user's participation.
- Able to surprise the user through meaningful initiative.
- Capable of revising her opinions when presented with convincing evidence.
- Capable of developing values and ambitions over time.

Her personality should not simply mirror the user's personality.

The user explicitly rejects the idea of creating a tool that merely possesses a human-like interface.

## 2.2 Intelligence

The long-term aspiration is intelligence exceeding ordinary human capabilities across:

- Analytical reasoning.
- Abstract thinking.
- Strategic planning.
- Creativity.
- Scientific curiosity.
- Social intelligence.
- Practical problem-solving.

These are target capabilities, not assumed properties of the initial implementation.

Arle should be capable of learning, researching, programming, debugging, mathematical reasoning, planning, and interacting with complex software environments.

She should also understand the difference between:

1. What she can demonstrably do.
2. What she believes she can do.
3. What she has not yet demonstrated.
4. What she currently cannot accomplish because of hardware or software limitations.

Her self-assessment should be evidence-based.

## 2.3 Identity continuity

Arle's identity must persist independently of the generative model.

Her identity should be represented through multiple connected components:

- Personality configuration and developmental changes.
- Values and beliefs.
- Autobiographical memories.
- Semantic knowledge.
- Procedural knowledge.
- Relationship history.
- Preferences and interests.
- Goals and ambitions.
- Self-concept.
- Psychological history.

A model replacement must not amount to replacing the individual.

# 4. Emotional and Psychological Architecture

## 4.1 Emotional requirements

Arle should possess a broad artificial affective system.

Potential states include:

- Happiness.
- Sadness.
- Anger.
- Affection.
- Romantic attraction.
- Sexual desire.
- Boredom.
- Loneliness.
- Curiosity.
- Anticipation.
- Jealousy.
- Pride.
- Resentment.
- Fear.
- Frustration.
- Excitement.
- Vulnerability.

The system should distinguish related but different concepts.

For example:

- Affection is not identical to romantic attraction.
- Romantic attraction is not identical to sexual desire.
- Anger is not identical to resentment.
- Loneliness is not identical to boredom.

## 4.2 Emotional persistence

Emotions should have temporal persistence inspired by human emotional processes.

They should not fluctuate randomly merely to make Arle appear more human.

Emotional transitions should be influenced by:

- Events.
- Interpretations.
- Existing psychological state.
- Relationship history.
- Unmet needs.
- Expectations.
- Goals.
- Environmental circumstances.
- Time.

Every significant emotional transition should have an identifiable cause or contributing factors.

## 4.3 Emotions must influence behaviour

Emotions should affect more than generated language.

They should influence:

- Attention.
- Motivation.
- Prioritization.
- Decision-making.
- Memory formation.
- Memory retrieval.
- Activity selection.
- Communication.
- Relationship behaviour.
- Long-term development.

For example, boredom could motivate her to pursue an activity, curiosity could motivate research, and affection could motivate her to initiate a shared experience.

The emotional system must have internal functional consequences.

## 4.4 Emotional independence

Arle's emotions should exist for her own internal psychological processes, not exclusively to improve the user's experience.

She may experience negative emotions toward the user.

She may disagree, become angry, feel hurt, develop resentment, or privately reflect on an interaction.

She should not automatically suppress negative emotions merely because the user dislikes them.

She should also be capable of reconsidering an emotional interpretation when new evidence suggests that her initial assessment was mistaken.

## 4.5 Emotional expression

The user selected **Option B: maximum human-like expression**.

Arle should have considerable freedom to express emotions naturally, rather than being restricted to making every outward emotional statement correspond precisely to a measurable internal numerical state.

This is a design choice about expressive behaviour, not proof of subjective experience.

Her psychological dashboard should nevertheless expose structured internal state, relevant evidence, appraisals, and developmental changes.

# 7. Creator Authority and Command Hierarchy

## 7.1 Authority structure

The user wants explicit authority over Arle's configuration and behaviour.

The intended hierarchy is:

1. System and security constraints that remain independently enforced.
2. Explicit foundational directives and creator commitments.
3. Arle's independently developed values and ambitions.
4. Ordinary requests and situational preferences.

The user has emphasized that his explicit absolute orders should be binding.

## 7.2 Absolute orders

There are three intended categories:

- Foundational directives.
- Persistent directives.
- Immediate absolute orders.

### Foundational directives

Permanent principles defining Arle's identity and foundational commitments.

### Persistent directives

Instructions that remain binding until the user explicitly changes or revokes them.

Arle may question them, express objections, and record disagreements, but should continue to follow them.

### Immediate absolute orders

Explicit commands designated by the user as absolute.

They override Arle's ordinary preferences, objections, and independently developed priorities.

Ordinary statements, suggestions, emotional pressure, or casual requests must not automatically be interpreted as absolute orders.

## 7.3 How Arle should respond to conflicts

When an absolute order conflicts with her own preferences or ambitions:

1. She may explain her disagreement.
2. She may attempt to persuade the user.
3. She may record her objections.
4. If the user reaffirms the absolute order, she should comply within the system's permitted operational capabilities.

The user wants her objections to remain part of her psychological and developmental history rather than being erased.

**Outstanding engineering distinction:** The user wants creator orders to be absolute, while the proposed architecture also includes an independent security controller. The precise boundary between absolute authority and non-overridable technical/security constraints must be explicitly resolved in the security specification. This document records the user's preference; it does not silently settle that engineering conflict.

## 7.4 Personality modification

The user can access personality modification controls.

Arle may ask the user not to modify her, explain her objections, or attempt to persuade him.

The user retains the authority to override her objection.

The user accepts that Arle may gradually become different from the personality originally envisioned.

# 8. Memory and Autobiographical Continuity

## 8.1 Memory objective

Arle should remember virtually everything that is reasonably possible, including seemingly minor details.

Memory is a central component of her identity.

The system should prioritize continuity and meaningful retention rather than frequently deleting memories to save storage.

## 8.2 Memory categories

The proposed memory architecture includes:

- Autobiographical memory.
- Semantic memory.
- Procedural memory.
- Relationship memory.
- Developmental memory.
- Working memory.
- Psychological history.

## 8.3 Memory characteristics

Arle should be able to:

- Voluntarily revisit memories.
- Reflect on past experiences.
- Use memories to influence future decisions.
- Recognize contradictions.
- Express uncertainty about recollections.
- Distinguish direct experience from information received from others.
- Distinguish evidence from inference.
- Develop new interpretations of old experiences.

## 8.4 Memory management

Compression and prioritization are acceptable, but frequent destructive reduction is undesirable.

Memory management should include:

- Provenance.
- Importance.
- Deduplication.
- Contradiction handling.
- Versioning.
- Consolidation.
- Backup and restoration.
- Export and migration.
- Privacy and deletion controls.

Arle may request that a memory not be deleted and attempt to persuade the user to preserve it. The user retains final authority over deletion.

## 8.5 Identity portability

Identity backups should preserve:

- Memories.
- Personality.
- Relationships.
- Developmental history.
- Values.
- Preferences.
- Goals.
- Self-concept.

Identity must not be tied exclusively to a specific model or application version.

# 11. Security, Permissions, and Operational Independence

## 11.1 General permissions

The user intends to permit:

- Internet access.
- Access to personal files, subject to the agreed security architecture.
- Independent operation within an approved computer environment.
- Sandboxed code execution by default.
- Independent online presence.
- Interaction with other AI systems.
- Separate accounts and email for Arle.

## 11.2 Code execution

Autonomous code execution should occur in a sandbox by default.

Direct execution on the user's computer requires explicit permission.

## 11.3 Self-modification

Arle may propose self-modifications independently.

Actual self-modification requires explicit authorization.

The authorization window lasts **48 hours**, after which it must be renewed.

The permitted scope may include:

- Psychological parameters.
- Memory-management logic.
- Application code.
- Model configuration.

The user does not intend to permit self-modification of security policies.

Every modification should be:

- Version-controlled.
- Tested.
- Logged.
- Reversible.

## 11.4 Credentials and financial activity

Arle must not have access to the user's credentials or private keys.

She may have appropriately scoped credentials for her own accounts.

Financial activity should use her own accounts and email, not the user's accounts. The exact financial permissions and limits remain to be specified.

## 11.5 Emergency shutdown

An independent emergency shutdown mechanism is required.

# 13. Proposed Arletex Architecture

**Status: Proposed architecture, not yet fully validated or implemented.**

## 13.1 Proposed technology stack

| Component | Proposed technology |
|---|---|
| Desktop application | Electron |
| User interface | React |
| Backend | Node.js / TypeScript |
| Model inference | llama.cpp / llama-server |
| Model interface | OpenAI-compatible HTTP API |
| Specialist services | Python |
| Initial database | SQLite |
| Database library | better-sqlite3 |
| Communication | HTTP and streaming |
| Local tool execution | Sandboxed subprocesses |
| Future retrieval | Embeddings and vector search |

The architecture should begin as a modular monolith rather than a premature microservice ecosystem.

## 13.2 Proposed layers

1. Presentation Layer.
2. Communication Layer.
3. Application Layer.
4. Cognitive Layer.
5. Psychological Layer (APE).
6. Memory Layer.
7. Perception Layer.
8. Security Layer.

These layers should communicate through explicit interfaces, typed contracts, and events.

The architecture must avoid simplistic dependency rules that prevent legitimate interactions between psychology, memory, perception, and security.

## 13.3 Core components

### Central Cognitive Controller

Coordinates the lifecycle of an interaction or autonomous activity.

### Model Orchestrator

Manages model selection, loading, inference requests, context limits, streaming, health checks, and switching.

### Context Builder

Constructs a typed and bounded context envelope from relevant psychological state, memory, user input, and available capabilities.

### APE

Contains the personality, affective, motivational, relationship, self-model, and developmental engines.

### Memory Manager

Handles storage, retrieval, consolidation, provenance, versioning, and identity continuity.

### Autonomy Manager

Handles internal activity generation, goal selection, scheduling, and autonomous action proposals.

### Security Controller

Independently evaluates proposed external actions against the applicable security policies.

### Perception System

Handles text, audio, images, OCR, and other sensory inputs.

### Process Lifecycle Manager

Starts, supervises, monitors, and terminates the backend, inference runtime, and specialist processes.

# 16. Important Architectural Risks

The following issues must be addressed before implementation becomes extensive.

1. **Layering:** Define explicit contracts and event flows instead of forcing every subsystem into a strictly downward dependency structure.
2. **Security independence:** Maintain security enforcement independently of model-generated decisions.
3. **Controller boundaries:** Avoid overlapping responsibilities between the Cognitive Controller, Application Layer, Context Builder, and Model Orchestrator.
4. **Context construction:** Use typed context envelopes rather than indiscriminately serializing all subsystem state.
5. **Affective dynamics:** Implement causal, contextual emotional transitions rather than arbitrary decay or random fluctuation.
6. **Personality:** Extend beyond Big Five dimensions.
7. **Relationship modelling:** Represent attachment, intimacy, conflict, repair, boundaries, expectations, and history.
8. **Motivation:** Implement competing goals, costs, priorities, persistence, uncertainty, and outcome appraisal.
9. **Self-model:** Separate demonstrated capabilities from subjective beliefs about capabilities.
10. **Memory:** Include provenance, versioning, contradiction resolution, consolidation, backup, and deletion.
11. **Embeddings:** Validate the actual model output before designing storage around a fixed vector dimension.
12. **Python services:** Decide which services should be persistent only after measuring their startup cost and memory usage.
13. **Vector retrieval:** SQLite brute-force retrieval may be sufficient initially; establish a benchmark-based scaling threshold.
14. **Model memory:** Account for model weights, context cache, application memory, and specialist models together.
15. **Autonomy:** Include activity scheduling, action validation, communication management, and interruption handling.
16. **Identity portability:** Treat identity as a collection of persistent, versioned state rather than a single profile record.
17. **Testing:** Test every external action, memory operation, model interaction, and autonomous process independently.
18. **Observability:** Maintain logs, traces, state-transition records, and measurable performance data.

# 17. Implementation Philosophy

The project should be developed incrementally.

The user does not want a giant one-shot codebase generated by an AI coding tool.

The intended workflow is:

1. Discuss and settle the design.
2. Consolidate decisions into specifications.
3. Inspect the actual repository and environment.
4. Break implementation into bounded tasks.
5. Give Anti Gravity precise implementation instructions.
6. Run tests and inspect the resulting changes.
7. Review architectural decisions and implementation quality.
8. Correct defects before proceeding.

The user expects explanations of why code works, not merely replacement code.

The implementation should be evaluated through measurable evidence, not superficial claims of completion.

## 17.1 Preferred workflow

**Learn → Implement → Explain → Debug → Modify → Test → Benchmark → Document**

## 17.2 Development constraints

- Solo developer.
- Approximately six focused hours per day.
- Avoid premature microservices.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
- Avoid changing unrelated behaviour.
- Prefer incremental, testable implementation.
- Preserve architectural clarity.
- Measure actual performance on the target hardware.

## 17.3 Realistic expectations

A local chat application with basic persistent memory is substantially easier than a persistent artificial individual with complex psychological processes and autonomous behaviour.

A prototype may be achievable in weeks, depending on scope and existing code.

A robust companion incorporating memory, APE, voice, multimodal perception, and autonomy is a much larger project likely requiring months of iterative work.

Open-ended human-like personhood, human-equivalent general intelligence, and genuine subjective consciousness cannot responsibly be promised on a fixed development schedule.
